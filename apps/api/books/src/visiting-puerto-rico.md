# Visiting Puerto Rico
*A practical guide for visitors, and for the people who help them*

Safehubby, 2026 edition. General information, not medical, legal or financial advice. Schedules, prices, rules and road conditions change: confirm with the airline, your lodging, official sources and the businesses you plan to use before you go.

## What this guide is

Puerto Rico is an island with a long coast, a rainforest, mountains, old cities and a warm welcome. It is also a place where a few practical details, such as how you get around, what the weather does, and how far you are from a hospital, shape how a trip feels.

This guide covers the basics a visitor wants to know before arriving, with extra attention to travelers who need more time, more comfort, or more support, and to the family members and helpers who plan for them. It does not recommend any hotel, restaurant or company. It is a planning guide, not an itinerary to follow exactly.

## 1. The basics

- **Puerto Rico is a territory of the United States.** For U.S. citizens flying from the mainland, it is a domestic trip. People who are not U.S. citizens follow the entry rules that apply to the United States, so check the requirements for your own situation before you travel.
- **The currency is the U.S. dollar.** Cards are widely accepted in towns and tourist areas. In small shops, rural areas, food stands and for some tolls and tips, small bills are useful.
- **Spanish and English are both official languages.** Spanish is the language of daily life across the island. In hotels, airports and tourist areas you will find English speakers. In smaller towns, expect Spanish, and a few friendly words go a long way.
- **Electricity is the same as the mainland United States:** 120 volts and the same plugs.
- **The island is in the Caribbean,** so it is warm all year, the sun is strong, and the humidity is high.
- **The emergency number is 911.**

## 2. Getting there

Most visitors arrive at **San Juan** (airport code **SJU**), the main airport for the island. There is also an airport in the west, at **Aguadilla** (**BQN**), and others that serve regional and local flights. Which flights are available changes with the season and the airline, so check the options for your dates.

A few things help:

- **Arrive with time to spare.** Both ends of a flight are busier than they look.
- **If you are traveling with someone who needs help,** ask the airline for assistance when you book and again a couple of days before. The guide *Traveling with an Older Parent* goes through how to do this.
- **Flying home to the U.S. mainland,** bags can be checked for items such as fresh fruit, plants and soil, and there is a security process before departure. Ask your airline how it works and arrive early.
- **Plan the ride from the airport** before you land. See the next chapter.

## 3. When to go, and the weather

The island is warm in every month. What changes is the rain, the crowds and the risk of storms.

- **The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30.** Storms are possible in those months, and are most likely later in the season. A trip in the season is not a bad idea, but it asks for more care: buy insurance that covers storms, ask your lodging about its plan, and follow the guidance of local officials and the National Weather Service.
- **Winter and early spring** (roughly December to April) are the busiest and often the driest, with higher prices and more crowds.
- **Rain is part of life,** especially in the afternoon and in the mountains and the rainforest. Bring a light rain jacket.
- **Heat and sun matter.** Plan the most tiring activities for the morning, rest in the middle of the day, drink water often, and use sunscreen and a hat.
- **Holidays** such as the Christmas season are long and lively on the island, and prices and crowds rise.

**Write down:** the dates, a plan for bad weather, and what your insurance covers.

## 4. Getting around

How you move around will shape the trip more than anything else.

- **San Juan and the metropolitan area.** Taxis and ride apps are common. Old San Juan has cobblestone streets and hills, and it can be hard for anyone with limited mobility; plan rides to the edge of the old city, and rest often.
- **The rest of the island.** Most visitors rent a car. Public transport is limited, and distances take longer than they look because roads are winding, especially in the mountains.
- **Driving.** Drivers drive on the right, as in the rest of the United States. Speed limits are posted in miles per hour while road distances on signs are often in kilometers, so pay attention. Mountain roads can be narrow, with sharp curves and rain.
- **Tolls.** Many highways have electronic tolls. Ask your rental company how tolls are handled and what it charges, so there is no surprise.
- **Fuel and parking.** Plan fuel before long drives in rural areas. In busy places parking can be tight and is sometimes paid.
- **Offline maps** help, since the signal can be poor in the mountains and on the coast.
- **Islands to the east.** Vieques and Culebra can be reached by ferry or by small plane. Schedules and departure points change, and seats on both can fill up, so check and book ahead.

For someone who cannot walk far, think about door-to-door transport and ask in advance whether a vehicle has a high step.

**Write down:** how you get from the airport to your lodging, and what you will use for the rest of the trip.

## 5. The island in six parts

These are broad impressions to help you plan. Details change, so check before you go.

- **San Juan and Old San Juan.** The capital, with old forts, colorful streets, restaurants, museums and beaches. Old San Juan is lovely on foot, and tiring for many. The metropolitan area is where most of the island's larger hospitals and services are.
- **The east.** Home to **El Yunque**, a national forest and rainforest. Access, trails and any reservation requirements can change, so check current information before you plan a visit. The east also has beaches and the departure points for trips to the smaller islands.
- **The south.** Includes **Ponce**, a historic city with its own character, and a drier, hotter climate than the north.
- **The west.** Known for beaches and surfing, and a slower pace. Includes the area around Aguadilla and Rincón.
- **The central mountains.** Cooler, green and quiet, with winding roads and small towns. A beautiful change from the coast, and not an easy drive in the rain.
- **Vieques and Culebra.** Small islands with beaches and a quiet feel. Services are more limited than on the main island, which is worth thinking about if medical care or specific supplies matter to you.

## 6. Planning for travelers who need more support

The island welcomes visitors of all ages, and a little planning goes a long way.

- **Pace.** Plan one main activity a day and leave room for rest. Heat and humidity tire people faster than they expect.
- **Where you stay matters.** Ask lodging about step-free entrances, elevators, bathrooms with seats and grab bars, and distance to dining. Get the answers in writing.
- **Medical care.** Larger hospitals are concentrated around the San Juan area, with others across the island. If your trip takes you away from the metropolitan area, find the nearest hospital and pharmacy before you go, and know how long it would take to get there.
- **Medicine.** Carry it in your hand luggage, in original containers, with extra and a written list. If you take medicine on a schedule, remember that the time on the island may differ from home.
- **Water and sun.** Drink water through the day, rest in the shade, and watch for signs of too much heat: dizziness, headache, confusion, very hot skin. These are signs to rest and get help.
- **Mosquitoes.** Mosquito-borne illnesses exist in the Caribbean. Use repellent, and ask a doctor or check current travel health guidance from official health agencies before you go.
- **Power and water.** Power and water services can be interrupted, particularly after storms. Keep phones charged, carry a power bank and a flashlight, and keep some water.
- **A person on the ground.** If you or the person you are traveling with would like someone to help, such as waiting with them, running an errand or arranging a ride, plan it in advance.

## 7. Safety and common sense

Puerto Rico is a place millions of people visit and enjoy. The usual rules of travel apply.

- **Keep valuables out of sight,** and do not leave anything in a parked car, particularly at beaches and trailheads.
- **Be careful in the water.** Beaches can have strong currents. Swim where there are lifeguards, follow the flags and the signs, and never swim alone. Ask your lodging about the beach you plan to visit.
- **Be careful on the road,** especially at night and in the rain.
- **Know the number:** 911 for emergencies.
- **Tell someone your plans,** and keep your phone charged.
- **If you are traveling in storm season,** follow the guidance of local officials and your lodging, and have a plan for where to go if told to.
- **Trust your judgment.** If a place or a person feels wrong, leave.

## 8. Eating, tipping and getting along

- **Food** is a pleasure of the island. Rice and beans, fried plantains, roast pork, seafood and strong coffee are part of daily life, and each town has its own favorites. Ask your hosts.
- **Tipping** is similar to the mainland United States. In restaurants a tip of 15 to 20 percent is common, so check whether service is already included. Tip drivers, porters and housekeeping as you would at home.
- **Greetings** matter. A "buenos días" or "buenas tardes" when you walk in is appreciated.
- **Pace.** Things can run slower than you are used to. Plan for it, and enjoy it.
- **Be respectful** of nature, of beaches and of the people who live there. Take your trash with you.

## 9. A flexible five-day plan

This is a sample you can change. It assumes a mix of rest and sights, and is gentle on energy.

- **Day 1: arrive and rest.** Settle in, eat something simple, take medicine, sleep early.
- **Day 2: a morning in San Juan.** See the old city in the cooler morning hours, have lunch, rest in the afternoon.
- **Day 3: nature.** A visit to the rainforest or a quiet beach, planned around the weather, with water and a hat.
- **Day 4: a different part of the island.** The south, the west or the mountains, with a driver or a rental car, and a long lunch.
- **Day 5: a slow morning and departure.** Pack the night before. Leave early for the airport.

## 10. What to pack

- Light, breathable clothes, a hat, sunglasses and strong sunscreen
- A light rain jacket
- Comfortable, closed shoes with good grip, and sandals or water shoes
- Insect repellent
- A refillable water bottle
- All medicine, in original containers, in your carry-on, with a written list
- Phone, charger and a power bank, plus a paper copy of important numbers
- A little cash in small bills
- Copies of identification, tickets and insurance
- A small flashlight

## Appendix B. Questions for your lodging

- Is there a step at the entrance? Is there a ramp or an elevator?
- How many steps are between the front door and the room?
- Is the bathroom step-free? Is there a shower seat and grab bars?
- Is the air conditioning reliable, and is there a backup if the power goes out?
- How far is the nearest pharmacy and the nearest hospital?
- What is your plan if there is a storm warning?
- Can you help arrange a driver or a doctor if we need one?
- What time can we check in, and what time must we leave?
